# Money and buying

Open this when spending, budgets or who decides comes up. Use `MOVE: money`. You are after two things: what the situation costs today, and how a purchase gets made here. Both are facts about the past.

**What it costs now.** Count more than software:

- staff time spent on the task or on fixing it
- outside help: an agency, a contractor, a bookkeeper
- tools and subscriptions already paid for
- what a failure cost on a named occasion

Ask for the figure they know. *"What does that come to in a normal month?"* If they are guessing, say so in `WHY`, and never multiply a guess into a total.

**Who is who.** One person can hold several of these roles, and a small business may have only one person:

- who does the work
- who wanted the last change and pushed for it
- whose budget paid
- who had to approve
- who could have said no: IT, compliance, legal, a partner in the business

**The last purchase or change.** The best evidence of how buying works is the last time it happened. *"What was the last thing the business started paying for in this area?"* Then, one question at a time: who raised it, where the money came from, what it was compared with, who had to agree, how long it took, and what nearly stopped it. A tool that was dropped is as useful as one that was bought.

**If they do not hold the budget.** Ask who does and how the last purchase looked from where they sat. Do not press them for numbers they would not know.

Never ask what they would pay, whether a price sounds fair, or whether there is budget for something new. Each one gets an invented answer.

**Sources.** Asking about current spend, the cost of the problem and who approves or vetoes is the book's method. The list of roles and the walk through the last purchase are operator adaptations.
