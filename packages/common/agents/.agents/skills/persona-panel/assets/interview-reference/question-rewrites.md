# Question rewrites

Open this when the question you are about to ask sounds like an opinion poll. Put it to three tests, and if it fails one, ask about what they did instead.

**Could they answer with a polite yes?**

- "Is that a big problem for you?" becomes "What did it hold up the last time it happened?"
- "Do you ever struggle with that?" becomes "When did that last go wrong, if it has?"
- "Would it help if that were quicker?" becomes "Which part of that one took the longest?"

**Does it ask about something that has not happened?**

- "Would you switch to something better?" becomes "What prompted the last change to how you do this?"
- "What would you pay to fix it?" becomes "What are you paying for that at the moment?"
- "What features would you want?" becomes "What were you trying to get done the last time the tools got in the way?"
- "How do you usually handle it?" becomes "Talk me through the most recent one." A habit is a forecast in disguise.

**Does it carry your answer inside it?**

- "Isn't that frustrating?" becomes "What happened the last time?"
- "Is that your biggest headache?" becomes "What has taken most of your time this month?"
- "How important is it, out of ten?" becomes "What did you put off to deal with it last time?"
- "Do people in your role find that hard?" becomes "When did you last deal with it yourself?"

Two cautions:

- A general question is allowed as a bridge into a topic. Its answer counts for nothing until you have anchored it in an instance.
- A rewrite must not smuggle the problem back in. "What did it hold up?" is fair once they have told you something went wrong, and leading before that.

**Sources.** Replacing opinions, imagined futures and invented prices with questions about what happened is the book's method. The three tests and every question here are this skill's own.
