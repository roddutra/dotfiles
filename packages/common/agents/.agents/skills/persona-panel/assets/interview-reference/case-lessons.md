# Case lessons

Open this when an answer surprises you and you are unsure what it means. Each pattern is a way an answer misleads, with the question that settles it.

- **A loud complaint, and nothing done about it.** It may already be handled cheaply. Ask who deals with it today and what that takes.
- **A habit that "always" holds.** Look for the exception: a busy season, time away, a new starter, an unusual client. Ask about the last time it slipped.
- **The task they name is not the goal.** Someone "writing up the notes" may really want cover if a decision is questioned later. Ask what finishing the task gives them.
- **One complaint, several causes.** Never looked for a fix, looked and found nothing that fitted, found one and dropped it: three different findings. Ask what they have tried.
- **A request that is really for someone else.** What they ask for may exist to satisfy a client, a manager or an auditor. Ask who receives it and what they do with it.
- **Top of a list they do not care about.** The worst part of a minor chore is still minor. Go back to `priority.md`.
- **Keen, and not the one who decides.** Ask who approved the last change of this kind, and who could have stopped it.
- **A real need with no budget behind it.** Need and money sit with different people. Ask who pays for what they use now.
- **They like the outcome.** Everyone likes cheaper, faster, more customers. That says nothing about what they do now. Return to an instance.
- **Your topic is not their problem, and another is.** Follow theirs. Label it with the nearest topic, or `open`, and say so in `WHY`.
- **A crude method that works.** A paper list or a plain spreadsheet can be enough at the scale they work at. Ask how much passes through it, then `accept`.
- **Nothing like what the role "should" say.** You may be talking to a different kind of customer. Ask what is unusual about their set-up. Do not average them in.

In every case one more question about what happened beats a theory about why.

**Sources.** Each pattern restates, in this skill's own words, what one of the book's worked examples teaches. None of the examples is retold. The questions, and the advice on labelling a reply, are operator adaptations.
