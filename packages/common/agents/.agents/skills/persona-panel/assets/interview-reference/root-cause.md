# Root cause

Open this when you have the facts of an event and want what lies under them, or when a thread that showed a cost, a workaround, a loss or a change of habit is about to end. Ask one at a time, skip what they have told you, and stop when an answer shows it does not matter to them.

1. **Why it matters** (`why`). What it put at risk, and what they were trying to achieve. *"Why did that one matter to you?"* *"What were you trying to get done by then?"*
2. **What it led to** (`consequence`). Take a consequence one step further: what that meant for the client, the week or the money. *"And what did that mean for the rest of the week?"*
3. **The hardest part** (`hardest`). *"What was the hardest part of that?"* The answer often names a different problem from the one they started with: follow it.
4. **What caused it** (`cause`). Why it happened, then why that happened, while each answer names something real. *"What made it come in that late?"* Stop at a cause they cannot change and do not mind, or at "that's just how it is".
5. **The other side** (`sequence` or `cause`). What they needed from the other person at that point and did not have, and what that person went through. *"What did you need from them by then that you didn't have?"*
6. **What changed since** (`alternatives`). A real problem usually leaves a trace: a new habit, a check, a tool tried. *"Have you done anything differently since?"*
7. **What would have helped** (`ideal`). Once, at the end of the thread. *"Looking back on that one, what would have made it go more smoothly?"* Then ask why of the answer. A tool, a feature or a product in the answer is a feature request: `feature-requests.md`.

**Signs you have reached the root:** they name something they care about for its own sake (a client kept, an evening back, money in hand), a cause recurs across events, or they have already spent time or money on it. **Signs it is not a problem:** no consequence, nothing tried, and no change since. That is a finding: take it and move on.

**Keep it theirs.** Ask why of their words, never of yours. "Why did that matter?" is open; "So that cost you the client?" puts an answer in their mouth.

**Sources.** Asking why of each answer, the hardest part and what a consequence led to is common practice in discovery interviews, beside the book's "why do you bother?" and "what are the implications of that?". The order, the moves and `ideal` are this skill's adaptations.
