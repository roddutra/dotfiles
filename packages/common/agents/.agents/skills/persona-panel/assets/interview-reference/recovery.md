# Recovery

Open this when an answer is praise, a generality, a promise, a feature idea or strong feeling, or when you catch yourself explaining. None of these is evidence. Each is a cue to get back to something that happened.

- **Praise** ("sounds great", "good on you for looking into this"). Sincere, kind or a way to wrap up: you cannot tell. Thank them in a word and return to their own work. *"Thanks. The last time you did this yourself, where did you start?"* Move: `deflect`.
- **A generality** ("always", "usually", "never", "all the time"). Ask for the newest instance and work from that one. *"When did that last come up?"* Move: `anchor`.
- **A promise or a hypothetical** ("I'd use that", "we're planning to"). Talk about the future is free. Ask what has been done already, and take "nothing". *"What have you done about it so far, if anything?"* Move: `deflect`.
- **A feature idea** ("it should just do X"). Do not discuss the idea. Ask what it is for. *"What would that have let you do last time?"* Move: `dig`. See `feature-requests.md`.
- **Strong feeling** ("nightmare", "I hate it", sudden energy). A lead, not a finding. Ask for the event behind it, then its consequence. *"What happened the last time it got that bad?"* Move: `dig`.
- **Still abstract after two tries.** Ask for one instance, step by step. If they cannot give one, say so in `WHY` and move on: the absence is the finding.
- **A comparison with another tool or method.** It shows how they think about the job. Ask how they used it and why they kept or dropped it. Never correct the comparison. Move: `alternatives`.
- **You are explaining, correcting or summing up.** Stop mid-thought and hand the story back. *"Sorry, I cut across you. You had just got it back: what happened next?"* Move: `sequence`.
- **The answer goes against what you expected.** Do not rescue it. Ask how it works instead. *"Right. How does that part get done at the moment?"* Move: `accept`.

In `WHY`, say what you set aside ("praise, not evidence"), so the record shows it was not counted.

**Sources.** Setting compliments aside, anchoring generalities and futures, digging under ideas and feelings, and stopping your own pitch are the book's method. The example questions and the move labels are this skill's.
