# Event reconstruction

Open this when a participant has named a real instance and you want all of it. Work down the list loosely: skip what they have told you, and leave it when an answer holds something worth following.

1. **Anchor** (`anchor`). Fix on one instance: the latest, or the one they just mentioned. *"Let's stay with that one. When was it?"* If none comes to mind, take "I don't remember". Never propose an incident.
2. **Trigger** (`sequence`). What set it going, and whether that was routine or an emergency. *"What kicked it off?"*
3. **Desired outcome** (`sequence`). What "done" meant: the job behind the task. *"What did you need to have in hand by the end?"*
4. **Sequence** (`sequence`). The first step, then the next, each finished before you ask for another. *"What did you do first?"*
5. **Tools and people** (`sequence`). What they used, who else touched it, who had the say. *"Who else came into it?"*
6. **Breakdown** (`sequence`). Where it got difficult, asked as an open question so that "it went fine" is easy to say and the story can still come. *"How did that one go from there?"*
7. **Consequence** (`consequence`). What changed because of it: a delay, rework, money, a client's reaction. Note in `WHY` whether a figure is a record or a guess. *"What did that end up meaning for the job?"*
8. **Coping** (`coping`). How it got done regardless, and whether that is good enough. *"How did you get it over the line?"*
9. **Alternatives** (`alternatives`). What they have searched for, trialled, bought or dropped, and why. *"What have you tried to change about it, if anything?"*
10. **Frequency** (`frequency`). How often, over a period that fits the work (a week for a daily task, a year for an annual one), and what got their attention instead. *"How many times did that happen last month?"*
11. **Constraints** (`constraint`). What stood in the way of another approach: authority, policy, other systems, the effort of switching. *"What stopped you doing it differently?"*

A full instance gives you a specific event, what it cost and what they did about it. Then find what lies under it: `root-cause.md`. Where one is missing, say so in `WHY`: no consequence, or no effort, is a finding. One instance is evidence about that instance, and says nothing about how often it happens to anyone else.

**Sources.** Following one real past event to its consequence, its workaround and what was tried is the book's method. The eleven ordered steps and their moves are an operator adaptation. A twelfth step, watching the work or seeing a real example, is left out: a simulated participant has nothing to show.
