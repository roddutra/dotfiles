# Priority

Open this when you do not know yet whether the topic matters to them. Find that out before you ask for detail. Detail about something a person does not care about reads like evidence and is worth nothing: they will rank the annoyances inside any category you hand them.

**Order of work**

1. **Go broad** (`priority`). Ask what has had their attention, with no area named. *"What has had most of your attention this past fortnight?"*
2. **Follow what they raise.** If they bring up the topic's area themselves, it is unprompted: say so in `WHY` and go to an instance.
3. **Name the area only if they have not** (`raise`). Use the topic's neutral name, and ask where it sits. *"Where does [the area] come, next to those?"* Whatever follows is prompted, and being willing to talk about it is not a sign that it matters.
4. **Decide.** Go deeper, or accept and move on.

**It matters to them when**

- they raised it before you did
- a recent instance comes easily
- it cost something they can name
- they have done something about it: a workaround, a search, money spent

**It does not when**

- no instance comes to mind
- "it's fine" or "you get used to it", and nothing was tried
- it is somebody else's job
- they answer politely and briefly, and add nothing

**When it does not matter,** ask one question to learn why: it rarely happens, the current way is good enough, or it is not theirs to fix. *"How does that get looked after at the moment?"* Then use `accept` and open the next topic. Do not ask again in other words, and do not explain why it ought to matter.

**Sources.** Establishing importance before detail, and treating a non-problem as a result, are the book's method. Marking prompted against unprompted and the two lists of signs are operator adaptations.
