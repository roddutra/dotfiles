Panel Report Kit

A head start on the visual summary of a simulated panel: one stylesheet, one helper script and the parts below. It is a baseline to build on and to change, not a template to fill in.

A starting point, not a rulebook

The kit exists so that a good report takes minutes. Use what helps, change what does not fit the project, and build new parts where the kit has none.

Yours to change
  • Colour, type and spacing. Where the project has a design system or brand guidelines, they win: replace the BRAND tokens, or restyle any part outright.
  • Which sections appear, and in what order. The recipes below are a usual shape, not a required one.
  • Which chart tells the story. Pick the form the data needs, or draw one the kit does not have.
  • Any component. Extend it, replace it, drop it, or write a new one for what this study found.
Keep these, whatever the design
  • The synthetic panel notice, word for word, directly under the title.
  • Counts of simulated participants with their base: "3 of 8". Never "most customers".
  • Quotations verbatim, each with its run and its question number or step.
  • Evidence against, and the limits, shown as plainly as the findings.
  • A "Go deeper" pointer to the markdown files, which stay the full record.
Using a project's own design system

Light touch: replace the BRAND block at the top of kit.css with the project's fonts, surfaces, ink and accent, and keep the rest. Full: drop kit.css and keep kit.js and the page structure (pp-page, pp-head with the notice, pp-section with an h2). Then two things need doing by hand. Define the --pp-* custom properties in the project's stylesheet, mapped to its own values, because the charts read their colours and fonts from them. And style the classes the helpers write: pp-table, pp-units and pp-u (r, n, b), pp-evid, pp-mark and pp-marks, pp-bars, pp-dotplot, pp-chart and pp-canvas, pp-fold, pp-stats, pp-persona, pp-legend, pp-chip, pp-callout, pp-sr, pp-tip, pp-theme, pp-toc and pp-lightbox.

What makes a page easy to take in

  1. The verdict first.Under the notice: the answer, how sure, and what to do next, in three short cells. Then up to three takeaways. A reader who stops there should still leave with the right picture.
  2. One idea to a chart, and the title says it."Concept A was first choice in 41 of 72 runs" beats "First choices". A chart nobody has to decode is worth five that need a legend studied.
  3. Plain words.No signal codes, no "exchange", no run ids in a headline. References such as P02-b, 6 sit beside the quote or the evidence, and the page explains them once.
  4. Detail on demand.Personas, model lists, the numbers behind a chart, timelines and long tables go in a fold. The page stays short and nothing is lost.
  5. Colour only where it means something.One colour, one meaning on a page (Tokens, below). The rest is ink on white, which also prints well.

Tokens

Two blocks at the top of kit.css. BRAND is yours to replace. CHART AND MEANING is a validated set: if you change it, run the palette validator again for both themes.

Colour means something: one colour, one meaning on a page
  • Series colours (s1 to s6) mean identity: who or what. On an interview page, s1 is "raised it, evidence for" and s3 "said it is not a problem, evidence against". On a review page they are the concepts, A, B, C in that order, wherever a concept appears: cards, charts, dots, quotes. On a usability page, the runs, only if they need telling apart. One page never gives a series colour two meanings, so a review page has no "for" and "against" colours.
  • Status colours (good, warn, crit) are for states only: a good move, a caution or limit, a blocker or a flag. Each comes with a shape and a word.
  • Neutral greys (n1 to n4) are for composition and for counts that carry no meaning: who was on the panel, runs by model, counts by kind. The helpers default to them: PP.panel's charts, PP.bars, and PP.columns with one series.
  • Indigo is structure: ranks, eyebrows, links, decisions. Everything else is ink on white. If a colour is not doing one of these jobs, take it out.

Charts

For a large panel, a chart carries what a table cannot. Drawn with Chart.js through helpers that take colours and type from the tokens, redraw on a theme change, once the web fonts arrive, and in light colours for paper. Every chart keeps its numbers in a fold underneath. Example data

The questionUseKeep in mind
How does one whole split?Donut, or pieFive parts at most, total in the middle. For more parts use bars.
How does a split differ between groups?Stacked barsSame order and colours in every bar. Lying down when the labels are long.
Which is bigger?Bars, side by sideStart the axis at zero. Three series at most beside each other.
What shape does each option have across the same measures?RadarFive to eight measures, three shapes at most. For exact values add the heat table.
How spread out are the scores?Dot plotWhen a small gap between means hides a large overlap.
A handful of people, one by oneSquares, not a chartUp to about 12 participants, each one visible.

Every chart helper takes title, note, height (in pixels, for the drawing area), unit (the first column of its numbers table), and a tone on each part or series, or a palette ("neutral", or a list of tones such as ["s1", "s3"]).

PP.donut PP.pieThe parts of one whole. Parts without a tone take the series order: give them tones, or palette "neutral", where they are not identities.
PP.stackStacked bars: the same split, compared across themes or groups. The tone both is the striped mix of for and against. Each segment's value is printed where it fits.
PP.columnsBars side by side, stacked upright with stacked, or lying down with horizontal. Each value is printed on the chart; values: false leaves them off. min and max fix the value axis, ticks is merged into its ticks (for example {stepSize: 1}), and a label may be an array of two lines.
PP.radarThe profile of a few options over the same measures. Read the shape, then check the numbers.

The panel, and things that fold away

Readers ask "who was this?" early and then stop caring. Give them the answer in a fold: closed it is one line, open it is everything.

PP.panelStat tiles, runs by model and personas by segment in neutral greys, every run of every pairing with its state, what every participant was also told, persona cards that open to the background the model was given, and every model with its exact id and its jobs.
PP.panel from the study filesThe helper's input is not study.json or runs.json as they are: segments are ids, runs.json calls the harness runner, keeps the study's alias in key and the exact model in model (empty for the configured default), and lists every job, not only the personas'. This is the whole mapping.
/* study = study.json, runs = runs.json, quant = analysis/quant.json, as the lead read them. */
const ROLE = { panellist: "plays the personas", interviewer: "interviewer", analyst: "analyst", critic: "critic of the questions", themes: "groups the notes into themes", writer: "writer" };

/* Personas: study.json gives a segment id; the panel shows its name. */
const personas = study.personas.map((p) => ({
  id: p.id, name: p.name, role: p.role, trait: p.personality, bio: p.bio,
  segment: study.segments[p.segment] || p.segment,
}));

/* Models: one row for each runner, exact model and effort that did any job. Where it played personas,
   its study alias (run.key) names the column of the matrix; an empty model is the harness's configured default. */
const byModel = new Map();
runs.forEach((r) => {
  const id = [r.runner, r.model || "", r.effort || ""].join("/");
  if (!byModel.has(id)) byModel.set(id, { runner: r.runner, model: r.model, effort: r.effort, key: null, jobs: new Set() });
  const m = byModel.get(id);
  m.jobs.add(ROLE[r.role] || r.role);
  if (r.role === "panellist") m.key = r.key;
});
const models = Array.from(byModel.values()).map((m) => ({
  key: m.key || m.model || m.runner, model: m.model, effort: m.effort, runner: m.runner,
  role: m.key ? "participant" : "other", job: Array.from(m.jobs).join(", "),
}));

/* Runs: the persona jobs only, with the alias as their model. A review or usability run carries its own state;
   an interview finished when quant.json lists it under runs.interviewed. */
const finished = new Set((quant.runs && quant.runs.interviewed) || []);
const panelRuns = runs.filter((r) => r.role === "panellist").map((r) => ({
  persona: r.persona, model: r.key, replicate: r.replicate,
  state: r.state || (finished.has(r.run) ? "done" : "incomplete"),
}));

PP.panel(document.getElementById("panel"), {
  title: "Who was on the panel", unit: "interview", personas, models, runs: panelRuns, told: study.context_notes,
});
