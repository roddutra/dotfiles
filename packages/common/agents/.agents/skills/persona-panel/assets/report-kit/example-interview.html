Do late payers cost tradies enough to care?

Synthetic panel: the feedback in this study was generated by AI personas, not by real people. It is not user research.

The answer
Chasing late payment looked handled, not painful. The sharper friction was paperwork, and the personas' backgrounds had planted it.
How sure
Low: 4 simulated interviews, invented histories, and one model carried the only unprompted payment signal.
Do next
Run the revised guide below with real tradies, and ask first whether today's invoice routine is already good enough.

The short version

  1. Chasing payment looked handled, not painful.Of 4 simulated participants, none raised payment without also calling part of it no problem: 1 said only that, and 3 did both. The usual story was a text or two, then paid.
  2. The sharper friction was paperwork, and the backgrounds planted it.Receipts for the electrician and re-entering job details for the plumber, each on both models. Both habits were written into the personas.
  3. The one payment consequence came from one model.A tight but covered wages week, told only by the plumber on sonnet. Without that interview, nobody brings up late payment unprompted.
What this cannot tell you
  • How common anything is. Every incident, figure and date was made up by a model.
  • Whether a tool would help or anyone would pay. No solution was shown.
  • A model is more agreeable and more articulate than a busy tradie.

Open it for the two invented people, what each model was told about them, and which model did which job. One persona stands for each segment, so segment and persona cannot be told apart here.

What each learning goal heard

Evidence against a goal gets the same room as evidence for it.

Goal 1

Does payment admin earn attention, next to everything else?

Heard

Attention went first to staffing, suppliers and organising the work. Receipt handling had concrete time costs in both versions of the electrician.

Heard against

Every version described payment follow-up as adequately handled. Only one brought up late payers before being asked.

Goal 2

What happens when payment comes late?

Heard

One or two text reminders and a look in the accounting tool. One account of two invoices past 45 days and a tight wages week.

Heard against

The wages were covered. No debt collection, no search for a fix. Exact counts and intervals were often not recalled.

Goal 3

How do they choose and pay for tools?

Heard

Xero or MYOB kept because it is familiar and the accountant knows it. Nobody could give the monthly fee.

Heard against

No recent software change was recalled by anyone, so nothing here shows an appetite to buy.

Themes, strongest evidence first

An analyst model coded the four transcripts into 51 notes and grouped them into themes. Only 13 notes tell of a problem or a workaround; the rest are background.

How to read the table
  • Raised it: told of something under the theme and never said it was no problem.
  • Said it is not a problem: said only that.
  • Both: called part of it no problem and told of something else there, which may be background rather than trouble.
  • Each participant sits in one group. The groups are never added together.

For the real interviews

The guide this rehearsal leaves you with. One question at a time, and accept "it's fine" as an answer.

  1. What has taken most of your attention at work over the last couple of weeks?
  2. Walk me through the last job you invoiced for.Follow what happened next. Do not assume it was paid late.
  3. Take me through the last time you sat down to do the paperwork.Tasks, time, consequences, and what they already do about it.
  4. When did a day last go differently from how it was booked?Accept that there may be no exception worth remembering.
  5. What did the business last pay for that was not materials or wages?Then who decided, what else was considered and what it cost, each asked separately.
Three goals to take in, the uncomfortable one first
  • Whether today's invoice routine is already good enough next to their other priorities, and what a recent exception cost, if anything.
  • Where the time went in the last admin session: reminders, receipts, re-entry.
  • How the last tool decision was made: who could refuse it, what else was considered, what was paid.

Still unproven

  • That the problem exists outside this panel.
  • That this kind of tradie puts it ahead of their other problems.
  • That a tool would fix it. Nothing was shown.
  • That anyone would pay. Nobody was asked.
