What blocked the runs

Both simulations reported a booking; neither had an independent success check. On the way, each hit the same three problems, ranked here by risk to real people.

  1. The big green button starts again. The real confirm is a small grey link. 2 of 2 runs found it only by reading closely, at steps 9 and 10. Both runs noted, stepping out of the persona, that the real Marg may click the big button, which loses the booking.
  2. "17 Oct 2026" was refused, and the error named no field. 2 of 2 runs guessed it was the date and retyped it once, at steps 7 and 8. The date box shows no format.
  3. "Booking confirmed" gives a reference and little else. No price, address, hours, deposit amount or word of an email, at step 10. The runs scored their own trust 5 and 6 out of 10.
Read these with every result below
  • An agent reads a page more thoroughly than a person and does not get bored, so real people will do worse wherever this task takes patience or close reading. Finding the small confirm link is exactly that.
  • Every result here is a hypothesis to test with real people, not a measurement of them.

What the debrief added

Each of the 2 runs was interviewed after it finished, from memory, with no browser. These are answers to an interviewer's questions, not things the runs raised unasked. They explain the stuck points above and add no finding of their own.

The four answers that explain a stuck point 2 of 2 runs debriefed, 4 questions each
P01-sonnet debrief, question 2

When the red box came up, what did you expect to see instead?

"Something went wrong, check your details" is no help. I'd checked my details, they were my details.

Explains stuck point 2 (T1 step 7, T1-7.png). The run had named the red box itself in its first answer, before the interviewer asked about it.

P01-sonnet debrief, question 3

After you changed the date, how long did you spend on that box before it went through?

It was more the annoyance of having to guess than the time it took. If it hadn't worked I'd have tried another way of writing it, but I'd probably have got cross and rung them.

Explains the single retry at T1 step 8 (T1-8.png). Here the interviewer named the moment first.

P01-sonnet debrief, question 4

What should I have asked you about the session that I didn't?

You could also ask about that little grey "confirm". I nearly missed it, and I think plenty of people would have clicked the big green button and lost the lot.

Explains stuck point 1 (T1 steps 9 and 10, T1-9.png). The question was an open closing one and did not name the link.

P01-codex debrief, question 4

I had a reference, but I still didn’t know where in Ballarat to collect it, what time they opened on Saturday or how much the deposit was.

In its first debrief answer, P01-codex said the first page offered collection from Ballarat. P01-sonnet never mentions it, and the synthesis says the step log cannot settle which is right. The start screenshot does: T1-0.png, the same image in both runs, reads "Pick up from our Ballarat depot, bring it back the next morning."

Both debriefs ended at the cap of 4 questions, with the second topic, the end of the booking, not opened. No script tallies a debrief and each run was asked different follow-ups, so there is no count here beyond 2 of the 2 runs debriefed.

What to change

  1. Make "Confirm booking" the primary button on the review page. Demote or rename "Start a new booking". Stuck point 1
  2. Say which field is wrong and show a date example. Mark the box, name the format beside "Pick-up date", and accept "17 Oct 2026". Stuck point 2
  3. Complete the confirmation page. Machine, date, price, depot address, opening hours, deposit amount, and whether an email is sent. Stuck point 3
  4. Clear the red banner when the person edits. Minor: it stayed up at T1 step 8 in both runs.
  5. Add a success check to the task next round, so a claim of completion can be verified.
Hypotheses to test with real people
  • People click "Start a new booking" and lose the booking. A five-second click test on the review screenshot would show it.
  • A written date such as "17 Oct 2026" fails for real users. Watch 5 to 8 people fill the form unprompted and count the formats and retries.
  • The generic error leaves people guessing, and some give up or ring. Record retries and abandonment in the same sessions.
  • Without a price, address, hours and an email notice, people doubt the booking. Ask afterwards: "Are you booked? What would you do next?"
Decisions for the product team
  • Which date formats the form must accept, and whether to add a date picker.
  • Whether a booking needs an explicit confirm step at all, or confirms on Continue.
  • Which details the confirmation page must carry, and whether an email is sent.

How far to trust this

What not to conclude
  • An agent reads a page more thoroughly than a person and does not get bored, so real people will do worse wherever a task takes patience or close reading. Finding the small confirm link is exactly that.
  • Every result is a hypothesis to test with real people, not a measurement of them.
  • This is thin: 1 persona, 1 task, 2 models, 1 run on each. It says nothing about other people or other tasks.
  • Two completed runs do not show the task is easy. The runs scored ease 5 out of 10.
Not measured
  • Completion: the task has no success check. 2 of 2 runs are complete, none stopped at a limit, and no check failed to run.
  • Whether the date format caused the error. Both runs changed it and got through, and the error never said.
  • The Prices and Help pages, whether an email is sent, and whether the form checks that the date is a Saturday or an open day.
  • The script's own stuck point (/book, steps 2 to 8) was partly rejected by the analyst: steps 2 to 6 are ordinary form filling.
