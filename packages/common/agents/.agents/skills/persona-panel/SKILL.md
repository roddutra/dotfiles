# Persona panel

Simulated customer research: AI panellists review concepts the way busy prospects would, attempt tasks in a running product (a usability study, below), or are interviewed about their work by an interviewer agent (an interview study, below), on more than one model, with bias controls. Each panellist is one headless process of a coding agent's CLI, started through a runner by `scripts/launch.py`, which also limits, tracks and resumes the runs. An interview is two such sessions, which the launcher carries turn by turn. Scripts do everything deterministic (scaffolding, capture, launching, validation, scoring, statistics, archiving). The lead (an agent on any harness, or a person in a terminal) designs the study, reads the results and reports; it never hand-builds these steps.

Scripts are in `scripts/` (Python 3 standard library; capture and every run that browses need the `agent-browser` CLI, capture also ImageMagick, and Poppler for PDFs; each runner needs its harness CLI installed and signed in). Every script takes the study directory and documents itself with `--help`.

## Study layout

```
<study>/study.json      lead config: concepts, personas, model entries, limits, factors, notes (copy assets/study.example.json)
<study>/study-brief.md  what was agreed with the user, hypotheses included (lead only; copy assets/study-brief.md)
<study>/key.md          label -> concept key and review orders (lead only)
<study>/forms.json      whether each files concept is images, PDFs or both (lead only; the analysis reads it)
<study>/runs.json       one entry per run and synthesis job: state, session, attempts, usage (an interview has two: its participant and its interviewer; a debrief adds its interviewer's)
<study>/followups.json  the follow-up questions asked, and how each run answered
<study>/prompts/        one launch prompt per run (per side of an interview, per interviewer of a debrief), one filled brief per job of the synthesis
<study>/panel/          the only folder panellists may read; in a usability study, panel/evidence/<run id>/ holds each run's step log and screenshots; in an interview study, panel/rooms/<run id>/ is the empty folder a participant starts in
<study>/interviewer/    interviews and debriefs: <run id>/, the empty folder each interviewer starts in
<study>/interviews/     interviews and debriefs: <run id>.jsonl, the turn log of each, and <run id>.md, its transcript (lead only; the launcher writes both)
<study>/accounts/       usability only: each run's test account with its secrets (lead only, never archived)
<study>/sites/          the site copies, in a study whose panellists do not browse (else panel/sites/)
<study>/analysis/       scores.csv, rankings.csv, runs.csv, answers.csv, lines.csv, checks, quant, usage.md, qual, synthesis; in an interview study transcripts/ (the analysts' copies), critique-<n>.md, notes.csv, critique.csv, themes.md and quote-check.md in place of the first five; a study that held debriefs has transcripts/ too
<study>/report/         the visual summary page: index.html (the lead's), kit.css and kit.js beside it, standalone.html (report_kit.py)
<study>/logs/           input and output of every process the launcher started
<study>/.launch.lock    the study's lock file: locked while a launcher has the study (one launcher per study), empty between launchers, never deleted
```

`<study>` stands for the study directory's path, which you choose and pass to every script. One study directory per round (`round-1/`, `round-2/`).

**Where the study lives.** By default under `~/panel-studies/<yyyy-mm-dd>-<neutral slug>/round-<n>/`, outside every repository. Confirm the place with the user when framing the study.
- Never inside a repository, gitignored folder included: a study holds harness sessions, the full input and output of every process in `logs/` and, in a usability study, test accounts with their secrets. Panellists could also wander into the product's docs.
- Keep it outside the concepts folder too.
- The slug names nothing about the product or the hypothesis, such as `study-a`: the full path is in every panellist's context.
- A study is working data, never committed. When the user wants it kept, `archive.py` copies a round into the research repo without its secrets and bulk, and the findings go into the product repo (`references/archive.md`).
- If your harness's sandbox can write only inside the project, hand the launcher's commands to the user to run outside it (`references/runners.md`, When the lead's own sandbox blocks the launcher). Never move the study into the project to suit the sandbox.

## Workflow

1. **Frame the study with the user.** What is being decided, who the target audience is, which concepts, what's fictitious or a placeholder (demo company, prices), what must stay local or uncommitted, and where the study directory goes (Study layout, above). Record what is agreed in `<study>/study-brief.md`, copied from `assets/study-brief.md`. It holds the team's hypotheses, so it stays beside `study.json`: `init_study.py` refuses to scaffold while a copy is under `panel/` or in a concept folder. Agree the scale: images dominate cost, about 20k tokens per concept page per run, and step 6 puts figures on it. Run a small panel early, after the first round of concepts, rather than after several rounds of iterating on judgement.
2. **Design the roster.** Read `references/personas.md`. Cover role x experience x personality, add 2 to 4 adjacent or out-of-scope personas on a wide round, and keep only the closest-to-target personas on a follow-up. Fix the full persona x model matrix now; adding models later means re-queuing.
   - **From a grid:** put a `roster` block (roles, personalities, stages, caps) in `study.json` and run `roster.py <study> grid`. It writes skeleton personas named `TODO`. Write each one's name and bio: `init_study.py` refuses a study with a skeleton left.
   - **From a roster file:** `roster.py <study> export --to <file>` and `roster.py <study> import --from <file>` move written personas to and from a JSON file kept in the project's own repository, so later studies reuse the same people under the same ids and results compare.
   - **Variants:** a persona with `"variant_of": "<id>"` is that persona with one trait changed, to test what the trait does. It reviews in its parent's order.
3. **Write `study.json`** from `assets/study.example.json`. A concept is a folder with `index.html` (`path`), a live page (`"kind": "url"`) or images and PDFs (`"kind": "files"`), and kinds can mix: `references/runners.md`, Concepts. Set `runner` and `models`: each entry is a model name on the study's runner, or `{"key", "runner", "model", "effort"}` (`references/runners.md` lists the runners). Set `limits` (minutes and budget for each run and each follow-up question, one pool for the synthesis), `synthesis.analyst` and `synthesis.writer` (there is no default), and `browse`. Set `context_notes` for everything a literal reader would misjudge, `find_the_answer` for the 4 to 6 questions a page must answer, `leak_terms` (internal vocabulary that must never appear in a review) and `forbidden_terms` (build notes that must never appear on a page). Add `states` for any toggle or switch that changes content, and `capture.media` if animations would be caught mid-way. Pin `labels` only when reproducing an earlier study. `replicates` above 1 repeats every run, as a stability check.
4. **Scaffold:** `init_study.py <study>`. It gives every concept a neutral label, copies the site folders under those labels (into `panel/sites/` only when panellists browse), and writes the instructions, persona files, run prompts, runs.json and key.md. No concept id, file name or URL reaches a panellist: `key.md` maps them.
5. **Capture:** `capture.py <study>`. No panellist run starts until every page and item of every concept is captured. A page or item that fails is listed, leaves an earlier capture as it was, and makes the script exit non-zero: fix it and capture again. A URL is captured from the live page as it is then, and a set of files becomes numbered items. Then look at one desktop sheet and one mobile sheet per concept yourself (one image and one document page for files): blank reveal bands, a repeated last screen, a missing page or a PDF cut short will be scored as real defects. Fix the concept or add a `context_notes` line, then recapture.
6. **Plan:** `launch.py <study> plan`. It starts nothing. Read all of it: every run and its limits, what each provider receives, what each runner lacks or could not switch off, the estimate, and the ceiling with its label (guaranteed maximum or estimate) and its caveats. Confirm the cost and the gaps with the user before any run starts. Exit code 1 means a runner cannot be used or `study.json` asks one for something it cannot do. When it says a runner cannot do a run from this shell, your shell tool is sandboxed: `references/runners.md`, When the lead's own sandbox blocks the launcher.
7. **Pilot:** `launch.py <study> pilot` runs the first persona on each model entry. Read those files as they land. Fix instructions or materials now, before the real runs, so no protocol change lands mid-panel: `init_study.py --force` (it resets every run and its recorded usage; the sessions the pilot started stay on record, so `purge-sessions` still deletes them), recapture if needed, and pilot again. Then run `plan` again: its estimate now comes from measured usage.
8. **Run:** `launch.py <study> run`, in the background with your harness's shell tool or in a terminal. It stays in the foreground until its runs end and prints one line per state change; `launch.py <study> status` shows every run's state, time and usage. After a usage limit, rate limit or capacity refusal (`blocked`) or an interruption (`partial`), `launch.py <study> resume` continues those runs in their original sessions. A run stopped by its own limit is `limited`: `resume --runs <ids> --extend` gives it one more allowance, and the plan's ceiling then no longer holds, so ask the user first. `launch.py <study> stop` stops the processes and leaves them resumable.
9. **Review as runs land.** Spot-check suspicious claims against the materials and log them in `analysis/lead-notes.md`. Report to the user at milestones, not per run (`references/communicating.md`).
10. **Validate:** `check_runs.py <study>`. A missing or unscored concept leaves its run `partial`, so `resume` sends it back. No command sends back a run that is `done`: fix a duplicated or invented section by hand while nothing is running, and log it in `analysis/lead-notes.md`. Record non-blocking flags; the analysts weight them.
11. **Score and analyse:** `extract_scores.py <study>`, `extract_answers.py <study>`, then `analyze.py <study>`. `extract_answers.py` tallies the find-the-answer verdicts, the forward test and the quoted lines, and prints how many entries it could not parse. Nothing is guessed, so read those rows in `analysis/answers.csv` and `analysis/lines.csv` yourself. Add segment views (`--segment a,b`) for the headline audience, a sensitivity view (`--exclude-model <weakest>`), and `--exclude-persona <id>` to keep a variant that only tests a trait out of the headline. Read "Model agreement by persona": unsettled personas are model taste, not segment signal. The output states "N of M runs complete" and lists the rest under "Incomplete runs".
12. **Qualitative synthesis:** hold any debriefs and ask any follow-up questions first (both below), then `launch.py <study> run --role analyst`, read `analysis/qual-*.md`, then `launch.py <study> run --role writer`. The launcher writes each job's brief to `prompts/` from the templates in `assets/`, tells each analyst which of its runs are incomplete, and saves each reply. Analysts wait for the panel unless `--allow-incomplete`. They never see the product's docs: put the decisions and constraints that apply now in a file in the study directory, outside `panel/`, and name it in `synthesis.context_files` (the study brief is refused there: an analyst who knows the hypotheses tends to find them).
13. **Review and report.** Read the synthesis yourself and check surprising claims against the persona files. Build the visual summary page from the report kit with `report_kit.py <study> init` and `bundle` (`references/report-page.md`). Then send the user the end-of-study message with the page: the headline, the ICP signal, what to change, what was used (`analysis/usage.md`), and the decisions only they can make (`references/communicating.md`).
14. **Next round** if the user wants one: brief designers with `assets/next-round-brief.md`, then run a follow-up panel (below).
15. **Archive** when the user wants it kept: `references/archive.md`. When they want the harness sessions gone, `launch.py <study> purge-sessions` lists them and `--yes` deletes them, which ends resume, follow-up questions and debriefs for those runs.

**Text-only pass (optional, before the full panel).** `"materials": "text"` in `study.json` makes a study a quick copy check: panellists read each page's `page-text.txt` and nothing else, and score no visual or mobile factor. Give it its own study directory, run the same steps, and never mix its scores with a full panel's: `references/method.md`, Text-only pass.

**One concept.** A study with one concept ranks and compares nothing: no ranking in the template, no leaderboard in the analysis, and scores with their spread across runs instead. Set `artefact`, add replicates, and report strengths and weaknesses rather than a level: `references/method.md`, Single-concept studies. `assets/study.files.example.json` is one.

**Usability study.** `"type": "usability"` tests whether personas can complete tasks in a running local build, not which concept they prefer: each panellist drives a browser, logs every step with a screenshot, and the analysis sets the completion each claimed beside what your success checks found, with the stuck points. Read `references/usability.md` first: tasks, test accounts, the protocol, safety and how much to trust the result. Start from `assets/study.usability.example.json`. The workflow is the same with these differences: there are no concepts and nothing to capture (skip step 5 and `extract_answers.py`), `launch.py <study> check` runs the success checks again (it keeps a verdict made with a test account that is gone, unless `--force`), and `launch.py <study> release --runs <ids>` lets go of the test account of a run you will not continue.

**Interview study.** `"type": "interview"` asks what problems these people have and how they cope now, before any concept exists: an interviewer agent holds a discovery interview with each persona, in the manner of the book The Mom Test, and the launcher carries every turn between the two sessions and writes the transcripts. The output is hypotheses and a rehearsed guide for interviews with real people, never evidence about real customers. Read `references/interviews.md` first: framing the round, the guide, models, the protocol, running, the analysis and how much to trust the result. Start from `assets/study.interview.example.json`, and agree `assets/interview-study-brief.md` with the user as the study brief of step 1. The workflow is the same with these differences:
- There are no concepts, labels or scores and nothing to capture: skip step 5, `extract_scores.py` and `extract_answers.py`. Follow-up questions and native mode are refused.
- `study.json` holds the guide in `interview` (`about`, `interviewer`, `goals`, `topics`, `turns`), `limits.interviewer` and `limits.turn` beside `limits.run`, and `synthesis.critic` beside the analyst and the writer.
- The interviewer follows each real event past its facts to why it matters and what causes it. A topic's `dig_for` lines tell it where to go deepest; prefer five or six topics with room to dig over many shallow ones (`references/interviews.md`, Depth over breadth).
- `pilot` holds one whole interview of the first persona on each model entry. Read its transcripts in `interviews/` before the rest. An interview that is `failed` at its first reply has a participant model too small for the role.
- `plan`, `run`, `status`, `stop`, `resume` and `check_runs.py` work by interview, each named by its participant's run id.
- The analysis is a chain of jobs and scripts, and each step refuses until the one before it stands: `launch.py <study> run --role analyst` and `run --role critic` (either order), `extract_notes.py <study>`, `launch.py <study> run --role themes`, `analyze.py <study>`, `launch.py <study> run --role writer`. To have a job done again, delete its output file and run its role again.
- `interview.py <study> quotes` checks every quotation of the synthesis against the transcripts. The writer's command runs it too. Never hand a synthesis over before it exits 0.

**Debrief interviews (optional, after a review or usability panel and before the analysts).** `interview.py <study> debrief --runs <ids>|all` has an interviewer question finished runs about the session each has just had, which is where questions about the user's own product belong. Add a `debrief` block to `study.json` (`interviewer`, `goals`, `topics`, `turns`) and read `references/debriefs.md` first.
- Each side of a debrief has `limits.debrief` (10 minutes and $2.00 by default), outside the plan's ceiling. Show the user `--dry-run` first: it prints the runs and the most they can use, and starts nothing.
- The same command again goes on with a debrief that stopped short. There is no `--extend`.
- No run's state, score or persona file changes. The transcripts land in `interviews/`, and the analysts' and the writer's briefs name them as product feedback, so debrief before `launch.py <study> run --role analyst`.
- A run on `pi`, a study in native mode and an interview study cannot be debriefed.

**Follow-up questions (optional, after the panel and before the analysts).** `followup.py <study> ask --runs <ids>|all --question-file <file>` puts one question to finished runs, each in the session it reviewed in, and each panellist appends a `## Follow-up <n>` section to its own file. Use it to probe what the reviews left unclear. Ask before `launch.py <study> run --role analyst`, so the analysts' and the writer's briefs name the questions. `followup.py <study> list` shows how each run answered.
- A follow-up never reveals another panellist's answer, a result, or what the team hopes to hear.
- It can only append that one section. Any other change is rejected and the file put back, so no score changes.
- Each run asked gets `limits.followup` for each question, outside the plan's ceiling. `ask` prints the most the runs can use, then starts. When a ceiling was agreed, show the user `ask --dry-run` first: it prints the same and starts nothing.
- To reach a run that was turned away or left the question unanswered, ask again: it becomes the next follow-up.
- The answers are qualitative evidence only: `references/method.md`, Follow-up questions.

**Native mode.** A study whose model entries name no runner is started by the lead with its own subagent tool and tracked with `runs.py`. It has no limits, no usage record and no isolation beyond instructions. Use it only when the lead's harness has subagents and no usable headless CLI, or as a fallback: `references/runners.md`, Native mode.

## Follow-up rounds

New concepts built from the findings get a smaller, cold panel: target personas only, at least 2 models, the same factors and questions so scores compare, and no knowledge of earlier rounds in any persona file, prompt or instruction. Add a new question to the template only when it answers something the last round couldn't.

## Rules for every study

- Panellists see only `panel/`. Nothing about the team's preferences, hypotheses or earlier results goes into persona files, prompts, instructions or an interview guide.
- Same review order across a persona's models; different orders across personas, except that a variant takes its parent's. `init_study.py` does this from the seed; don't hand-edit orders. When it warns that every persona got the same order, set another `seed` and scaffold again before any run.
- Launch prompts are the files in `prompts/`, verbatim. The launcher sends them as they are; edits drift between runs and break comparability.
- A study is all runners or all native mode, never a mix.
- A live URL is browsed only when `study.json` says `"browse": true`. Agree it with the user first: visits reach the site, and only the instructions stop a panellist submitting a form.
- Model keys are letters and digits only, and unique within a study: file names and run ids split on hyphens.
- `run` starts only `pending` and `failed` runs. It never restarts a `partial` or `limited` run, and neither should you: continue it with `resume`.
- Concepts of different forms (a PDF, a screenshot, a captured page) are compared partly on format. When `quant.md` opens with a format caution, compare Visual design and Mobile within one form only, and say so when reporting.
- Incomplete runs stay out of the headline tables. Report their scores only as what the "Incomplete runs" table shows.
- A usability study uses throwaway test accounts on a local build, never real credentials or a real customer's account. A remote target is read-only and needs the user's agreement.
- A test account's secrets never appear in a persona file, a step log, the synthesis or the archive. `check_runs.py` and `archive.py` scan for them: fix what they name, never work around it.
- A standalone interview never names the user's own product. Its name and the team's words for its idea go in `leak_terms`, and questions about it belong in a debrief.
- Both sides of an interview need a mid-tier model or larger, never a vendor's smallest and fastest tier, and the interviewer the strongest model available. That holds for a debrief's interviewer too. Its participant is the run's own model, and the plan prints the tier line for an interview study only.
- A count of simulated participants is never reported as frequency or prevalence: say "3 of 8 simulated participants".
- Treat the output as a hypothesis to test with real people, and say so when reporting. Simulated readers read more than real ones, and an agent is more patient with a task than a person is.
- Every report and summary carries the synthetic panel notice under its title, word for word: "Synthetic panel: the feedback in this study was generated by AI personas, not by real people. It is not user research." The scripts put it in the persona files, the transcripts, the synthesis and the archive (`references/communicating.md`).

Read `references/method.md` before launching runs: it covers when to run, runners and model choice across vendors, limits and the plan, piloting, what each run state means, capture pitfalls, concept kinds, single-concept studies, concept design for panels, replicates and variants, answer tallies, follow-up questions, and how much to trust each result. Read `references/usability.md` before a usability study, `references/interviews.md` before an interview study, and `references/debriefs.md` before debriefing the finished runs of a review or usability study.
